There are nineteen Disney t-shirts in my wife's closet.

I counted them last week. We were packing for our next trip, and she was holding up two shirts — a glittery Magic Kingdom castle situation and a more subdued floral Mickey silhouette — asking which one was more "first-park-day energy."

I said both. She agreed and packed both.

This isn't a complaint. It's an observation. Somewhere between our first Disney trip and now, my wife developed a Disney t-shirt collection that has its own gravitational pull. Each one was bought with conviction. Each one tells a story. Most of them have made the trip at least once. None of them are getting donated.

So in the interest of family transparency — and because I genuinely think other Disney parents will recognize themselves in this — here's a complete accounting of our t-shirt situation.

## What This Says About Us

I used to roll my eyes at the volume. I don't anymore.

Each shirt represents a moment my wife was excited enough about an upcoming trip to want a specific outfit for it. Each one was selected with intention. The Belle tee for the day we'd be near the bookstore. The sequin castle for fireworks night. The Nervous Rex for the early morning park day she knew would be hard.

She's not buying Disney t-shirts because she has nothing better to do with the money. She's buying them because she's planning. The shirts are how she stays excited between trips. They're how she anchors a Disney memory to something tangible after we're home.

Nineteen shirts in. She just added a twentieth to her Amazon cart. I didn't say anything about it.

> **The Honest Take:** If you're rolling your eyes at this article and thinking "no one needs nineteen Disney t-shirts," you're not wrong, technically. But you're also missing the point. The shirts are about the planning, not the wearing. If your spouse builds their excitement for trips through small purchases, embrace it. The alternative is a partner who's bored of Disney by trip three, and that's much worse.
